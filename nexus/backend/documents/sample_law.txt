Cybercrime in Nepal refers to criminal activities involving computers, digital systems, or networks. It includes hacking, phishing, identity theft, and online fraud.

Unauthorized access to computer systems is a punishable offense under Nepal’s cyber law framework.

Online fraud involves deceiving individuals or organizations using digital platforms to steal money or sensitive information.

The Electronic Transactions Act of Nepal governs cybercrime-related offenses and provides legal procedures for digital security and prosecution.

Punishments for cybercrime in Nepal depend on the severity of the offense and may include fines, imprisonment, or both.

A contract is a legally enforceable agreement between two or more parties that creates mutual obligations.

For a contract to be valid, it must include offer, acceptance, lawful consideration, and free consent of parties.

Breach of contract occurs when one party fails to fulfill its obligations as agreed in the contract.

Remedies for breach of contract include compensation for damages, specific performance, or cancellation of the contract.

---

Criminal law deals with offenses against society or the state such as theft, assault, fraud, and murder.

Punishment in criminal law may include imprisonment, fines, community service, or probation depending on severity.

The criminal justice process includes investigation, arrest, trial, and sentencing.

Law enforcement agencies are responsible for maintaining public order and investigating crimes.

---

Civil law deals with disputes between individuals or organizations over rights and obligations.

Property disputes are resolved under civil law when ownership or usage rights are contested.

Family law, including marriage, divorce, and inheritance, is part of civil law.

Civil cases focus on compensation and dispute resolution, not criminal punishment.

---

Constitutional law defines the structure of government and fundamental rights of citizens.

Fundamental rights include freedom of speech, equality before law, and right to privacy.

The constitution establishes powers and responsibilities of government institutions.

Courts can invalidate laws that violate constitutional rights.
